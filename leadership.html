My Leadership Story

From the Kitchen to the Classroom to Learning Leadership

I learned leadership in environments where preparation, people, quality, timing, resources, and execution all mattered. As an Executive Chef, I led teams through high-pressure operations while developing people, solving problems, managing resources, and delivering experiences that had to work in the real world.

That experience taught me that leadership is both strategic and deeply human. A good plan is only as strong as the people, communication, systems, and execution behind it.

When I moved into instructional design and learning technology, I brought that mindset with me. I learned to look beyond the individual course or learning object and consider the larger system: the people involved, the process behind the work, the technology supporting it, and the experience ultimately delivered to the learner.

Today, I bring those perspectives together to lead learning initiatives that are thoughtful, practical, scalable, and grounded in real organizational needs.

Project Leadership

Turning Complex Work Into Manageable Steps

I approach project management as a leadership responsibility, not simply a tracking exercise. My role is to create clarity, anticipate challenges, align people around the outcome, and keep meaningful work moving from strategy to implementation.

Define

Clarify the problem, desired outcomes, stakeholders, scope, and measures of success.

Plan

Establish priorities, dependencies, timelines, resources, and practical workflows.

Align

Keep teams and stakeholders informed, engaged, and moving toward the same outcome.

Execute

Remove barriers, solve problems, manage dependencies, and keep deliverables moving.

Evaluate

Review outcomes, gather feedback, identify lessons, and determine what should change.

Improve

Turn lessons learned into better processes, standards, resources, and experiences.

Instructional Design Practice

Theory Should Influence the Design—Not Just the Vocabulary

Instructional design frameworks give me a structure for making better decisions. I use them as practical tools for solving learning problems—not simply concepts to list on a résumé.

Backward Design

Start with the desired outcome, then determine the evidence, activities, content, and experience needed to get there.

ADDIE

Use analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation as a practical framework for iterative learning development.

Bloom's Taxonomy

Align activities and assessments with the level of thinking learners need to demonstrate.

Accessibility

Build accessibility and usability into the experience from the beginning rather than treating them as final-stage fixes.

Continuous Improvement

Prepare. Execute. Learn. Improve.

I believe strong leaders stay curious. My "1% better" mindset means looking for meaningful improvements in how teams work, how systems operate, and how learners experience the results. Small improvements compound into stronger processes, better experiences, and more sustainable outcomes.

Leadership Philosophy

Develop People. Improve Systems. Create Better Experiences.

Leadership isn't about having all the answers. It's about creating the conditions for people and ideas to succeed, making thoughtful decisions, and having the discipline to keep improving what comes next.
