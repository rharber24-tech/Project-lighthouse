The Challenge

Exploring where AI can create value without replacing human judgment.

AI creates exciting opportunities to improve assessment workflows, but efficiency alone does not define a successful learning solution. Any approach also has to consider instructional quality, feedback, transparency, learner experience, and the role of the educator.

The opportunity was to explore where AI could reduce repetitive work while keeping meaningful instructional decisions in human hands.

This required looking at the workflow as a whole—where AI could assist, where human judgment remained essential, and how the experience could be evaluated before broader adoption.

Strategic Approach

Innovating with purpose, not technology for technology's sake.

The approach focused on identifying meaningful opportunities for AI, designing around the existing assessment workflow, and evaluating the experience through both instructional and human-centered lenses.

Identify the Opportunity

Examined assessment workflows to identify repetitive tasks and opportunities where AI could potentially reduce administrative burden without reducing instructional quality.

Design the Workflow

Considered how AI could fit into the existing process rather than treating the technology as a solution in isolation.

Protect Human Judgment

Kept educators and instructional expertise central to decisions involving quality, feedback, evaluation, and learner support.

Evaluate Responsibly

Considered quality, transparency, learner experience, and responsible adoption before treating AI as a scalable solution.

Responsible AI

Innovation requires intentional guardrails.

Effective AI adoption in education requires more than technical capability. It requires clear expectations around quality, transparency, responsibility, and human oversight.

Human in the Loop

AI can assist the process, but meaningful instructional decisions should remain accountable to people.

Quality Matters

Efficiency is valuable only when the resulting assessment and feedback maintain instructional quality.

Transparency

Learners and educators should understand where AI contributes to an assessment experience.

Learner-Centered

Technology should improve the learning experience—not simply make the underlying workflow faster.

Instructional Design Lens

AI should make good instructional design stronger.

AI does not replace instructional design. It creates new possibilities for how instructional designers approach repetitive tasks, content development, feedback, and learner support.

The same principles that guide effective learning design still matter: clear objectives, meaningful practice, appropriate cognitive demand, accessibility, feedback, and alignment between learning activities and outcomes.

The technology becomes valuable when it supports those principles rather than distracting from them.

Leadership Takeaway

The goal isn't to use AI. The goal is to use it well.

Effective innovation starts with a meaningful problem, not a new technology. My approach to AI focuses on understanding the workflow, protecting the learner experience, supporting educators, and determining where technology can create genuine value.
