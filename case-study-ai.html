The Challenge

Exploring AI without losing the human element of assessment.

Assessment is one of the most important and resource-intensive parts of the learning experience. Instructors need meaningful opportunities to evaluate learner work, provide feedback, and identify opportunities for growth.

At the same time, emerging AI technologies create opportunities to rethink how assessment workflows can be supported without replacing instructor judgment.

The challenge was not simply determining whether AI could evaluate learner work. The more important question was how AI could be introduced in a way that preserved instructional quality, human oversight, and meaningful feedback.

Strategic Approach

Designing AI around the learning experience.

The approach focused on understanding the instructional problem first, then evaluating where AI could provide meaningful support.

Start With the Problem

Evaluated the assessment challenge before selecting technology, keeping instructional goals at the center of the solution.

Evaluate AI Workflows

Explored how AI could support assessment workflows while identifying opportunities, limitations, and areas requiring human review.

Preserve Human Oversight

Positioned AI as an instructional support rather than a replacement for instructor expertise, judgment, and feedback.

Pilot and Refine

Used an iterative approach to examine workflow quality, usability, and opportunities for improvement before broader adoption.

Responsible AI

Innovation requires thoughtful boundaries.

The goal was not to automate assessment simply because the technology made automation possible. The goal was to identify where AI could create value while protecting the quality of the learning experience.

Human Judgment

Instructor expertise remains central to evaluating context, nuance, learner progress, and the quality of feedback.

Transparency

AI-supported processes should be understandable to the people using them and the learners affected by them.

Accessibility

Technology decisions should continue to support inclusive learning experiences and equitable participation.

Continuous Evaluation

AI implementations require ongoing evaluation as workflows, technologies, and learning needs evolve.

Impact

Moving from AI experimentation to strategic innovation.

The project created an opportunity to examine AI not simply as a new technology, but as a potential component of a broader learning strategy.

Leadership Takeaway

The best AI strategy starts with better learning design.

Emerging technology creates possibilities, but instructional strategy determines whether those possibilities create meaningful value. My approach is to evaluate AI through the lens of learning science, learner experience, accessibility, human judgment, and organizational strategy.
