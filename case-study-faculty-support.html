The Challenge

Technology only creates value when people can use it effectively.

Learning platforms and digital tools provide powerful capabilities, but technology alone does not create better learning experiences.

Faculty and instructional teams need clear, accessible, and practical support to translate those capabilities into effective teaching and learning practices.

The challenge was therefore broader than technology implementation. It was about creating a support ecosystem that could help people understand, adopt, and use learning technology with confidence.

Strategic Approach

Designing support around the people using the system.

Across institutional and consulting environments, the work focused on connecting technology, instructional design, and human support rather than treating them as separate functions.

Enable Faculty

Created practical resources that helped faculty understand learning technologies and apply them within their instructional practice.

Scale Digital Support

Developed instructional videos, tutorials, and digital resources that could support recurring faculty and technology needs.

Consult Strategically

Applied instructional design expertise to consulting engagements, helping stakeholders identify practical approaches to learning challenges.

Design for Scale

Built resources and processes with reuse, consistency, accessibility, and long-term sustainability in mind.

Institutional Scale

Supporting learning at enterprise scale with Canvas.

Through instructional design consulting work with Rowan University, I supported learning experiences within a large-scale Canvas environment serving approximately 1,100 courses per term and 15,000 students.

Working at this scale reinforced the importance of consistent processes, clear documentation, accessible resources, and support strategies that can serve diverse faculty and learner needs.

The experience strengthened my ability to think beyond individual courses and consider how instructional design and learning technology function as part of a larger institutional ecosystem.

Instructional Design Consulting

Bringing instructional design expertise beyond one organization.

My instructional design consulting work with Luther College provided another opportunity to apply learning strategy and design expertise in a different higher education environment.

Consulting requires listening carefully to stakeholders, understanding the context behind a learning challenge, and translating instructional design principles into practical recommendations.

The experience reinforced an important part of my approach: effective instructional design begins with understanding the problem before selecting the solution.

Leadership Takeaway

The best technology strategy is built around people.

Learning technology creates meaningful value when organizations invest not only in platforms, but also in the people, processes, resources, and support structures required to use those platforms well.
