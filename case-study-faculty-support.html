The Challenge

Making learning support easier to find, understand, and use.

Faculty and learning teams often work across multiple platforms, processes, tools, and instructional expectations. When support is scattered or inconsistent, even capable people can spend unnecessary time figuring out how to accomplish routine work.

The opportunity was to move beyond one-off assistance and create reusable resources, standardized workflows, and intuitive learning support experiences that could scale across programs and teams.

The work required more than creating individual job aids or videos. It required understanding recurring needs, identifying opportunities for standardization, and designing resources that could make people more confident and independent.

Strategic Approach

Designing support around real people and real workflows.

I approached faculty support as a learning-experience problem. The goal was to make the right information easier to access while creating reusable resources that could improve consistency across the organization.

Design Around Real Needs

Start with the faculty and learner experience. Identify recurring questions, friction points, workflow challenges, and opportunities for improvement.

Standardize What Matters

Create repeatable structures, templates, job aids, and processes that improve consistency without removing the flexibility needed for good teaching.

Make Learning Support Engaging

Turn technical or procedural information into clear, approachable, and engaging learning experiences rather than simply documenting steps.

Improve Through Feedback

Use testing, stakeholder feedback, and real-world use to continuously refine resources, workflows, and support experiences.

The Support Ecosystem

Give people the right resource at the right moment.

Effective support combines multiple forms of guidance. Different problems require different resources, so the ecosystem was designed to meet people where they are.

How-To Videos

Short, focused visual guidance for technology, workflows, and common tasks.

Job Aids

Practical reference materials that help people complete tasks accurately and confidently.

Templates

Reusable structures that improve consistency while reducing unnecessary development time.

Learning Resources

Instructional materials that turn processes, standards, and technology into usable learning experiences.

Instructional Design Lens

Support experiences should still be learning experiences.

Faculty support is most effective when resources are intentionally designed around outcomes, cognitive demand, usability, accessibility, and the real context in which people will use them.

Backward Design

Begin with the outcome. What should the user be able to accomplish after interacting with the resource?

Bloom's Taxonomy

Match the resource to the level of thinking or performance required rather than providing information without purpose.

Multimedia Principles

Use visuals, narration, structure, and concise presentation intentionally to reduce cognitive load and improve comprehension.

Accessibility & Usability

Design resources so they are clear, navigable, accessible, and useful across different contexts and abilities.

Project Leadership

Support systems don't happen without disciplined execution.

Building an effective support ecosystem requires the same project leadership principles as larger learning initiatives: define the need, prioritize the work, coordinate contributors, establish standards, deliver resources, and continuously improve.

I use structured workflows to move initiatives from idea to implementation while maintaining visibility into scope, dependencies, quality, deadlines, and stakeholder needs.

02

03

Coordinate

04

Develop

Create resources that are useful, accessible, engaging, and easy to maintain.

05

Implement

Make resources available where people actually need them and support adoption.

06

Improve

Use feedback and recurring needs to strengthen resources, processes, and the support system.

Impact

Creating support that scales beyond one person or one question.

The work created a stronger foundation for supporting faculty and learning teams through reusable resources, consistent practices, and accessible learning experiences. The result is a support model designed to build individual confidence while improving consistency across learning work.

Scalable Reusable resources that support recurring needs
Consistent Shared standards and practices across learning work
Engaging Support experiences designed to be clear, accessible, and useful
Leadership Takeaway

The best support systems make people feel capable.

Effective enablement is not about creating more documentation. It is about reducing friction, building confidence, and creating systems that help people succeed without having to reinvent the process every time.
