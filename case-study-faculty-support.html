The Challenge

Support shouldn't depend on who knows where to look.

Faculty and learning teams often work across multiple platforms, processes, tools, and instructional expectations. Without a cohesive support ecosystem, even simple tasks can become time-consuming and inconsistent.

The opportunity was to move beyond one-off assistance and create reusable resources, standardized workflows, and intuitive learning support experiences that could scale across programs and teams.

Strategic Approach

Build the system, not just the solution.

Design Around Real Needs

Start with the faculty and learner experience. Identify recurring questions, friction points, workflow challenges, and opportunities for improvement.

Standardize What Matters

Create repeatable structures, templates, job aids, and processes that improve consistency without removing the flexibility needed for good teaching.

Make Learning Support Engaging

Turn technical or procedural information into clear, approachable, and engaging learning experiences rather than simply documenting steps.

Improve Through Feedback

Use testing, stakeholder feedback, and real-world use to continuously refine resources, workflows, and support experiences.

The Ecosystem

A connected collection of support experiences.

Effective support is rarely one resource. It is an ecosystem of experiences that meet people where they are and provide the right level of guidance at the right time.

My work has included developing and coordinating resources such as how-to videos, job aids, templates, process documentation, course resources, and other learning technologies.

How-To Videos

Short, focused resources that demonstrate processes and reduce the need for repetitive one-on-one support.

Job Aids

Practical reference materials designed to help users complete tasks quickly and confidently.

Templates

Reusable structures that create consistency while giving teams a strong foundation for their work.

Learning Resources

Instructional experiences that teach concepts rather than simply presenting instructions.

Instructional Design Lens

Support experiences should still be learning experiences.

Backward Design

Begin with the outcome. What should the user be able to accomplish after interacting with the resource?

Bloom's Taxonomy

Match the resource to the level of thinking or performance required rather than providing information without purpose.

Multimedia Principles

Use visuals, narration, structure, and concise presentation intentionally to reduce cognitive load and improve comprehension.

Accessibility & Usability

Design resources so they are clear, navigable, accessible, and useful across different contexts and abilities.

Project Management

Good ideas only matter when they get delivered.

Creating a support ecosystem requires more than instructional design. It requires project management, prioritization, communication, stakeholder coordination, testing, and follow through.

I use structured workflows to move initiatives from idea to implementation while maintaining visibility into scope, dependencies, quality, deadlines, and stakeholder needs.

Designing for Scale

The goal isn't more support. It's better support.

Scalable

Reusable resources and standardized processes reduce duplicated effort and support larger populations without requiring proportional increases in support.

Consistent

Shared standards create a more predictable experience across courses, programs, teams, and platforms.

Engaging

Well-designed support can be useful, approachable, and even enjoyable—turning required processes into better learning experiences.

Leadership Takeaway

The best support systems make people feel capable.

Instructional design leadership isn't only about creating learning. It's about creating the conditions that allow other people to teach, learn, create, and succeed.
