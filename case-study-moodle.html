Leading instructional design strategy, cross-functional coordination, testing, standardization, and implementation during a major LMS modernization effort—connecting platform change to the people, processes, and learning experiences it supports.

Role Instructional Design Leadership
Focus LMS Modernization
Platform Moodle
Strategy Systems · UX · Standardization
The Challenge

Modernizing the platform without disrupting the learning experience.

An LMS modernization is more than a technical upgrade. Changes to the platform can affect course structure, navigation, faculty workflows, instructional resources, and ultimately the student experience.

The Moodle 4.x modernization effort required coordination across Instructional Design, IT, Academics, Content Development, and other stakeholders. The work involved testing workflows, evaluating changes, identifying issues, and preparing learning environments for implementation.

My focus was connecting the technical transition to the instructional and operational realities surrounding it—helping ensure that modernization improved the environment rather than simply changing it.

Strategic Approach

Leading change across the learning ecosystem.

I approached the modernization as an organizational change initiative, not simply a platform update. The work required coordination, testing, standardization, communication, and an ongoing focus on how the changes would affect faculty, staff, and learners.

Coordinate the Work

Connected Instructional Design, IT, Academics, Content Development, and other stakeholders to organize priorities, dependencies, decisions, and implementation activities.

Test Before Launch

Tested courses and workflows in development environments to identify functionality, formatting, usability, and implementation issues before broader rollout.

Standardize the Experience

Applied consistent approaches across courses, templates, instructional resources, and support materials to reduce unnecessary variation and create a more predictable experience.

Protect the Learner Experience

Evaluated changes through the lens of clarity, accessibility, usability, faculty workflow, and student success—not simply whether the technology functioned.

Instructional Design Strategy

Applying instructional design at the system level.

The modernization work provided an opportunity to apply instructional design thinking beyond individual courses. I used learning science, accessibility, usability, and learner-centered principles to inform decisions about how the larger learning environment should function.

Backward Design

Kept intended learning outcomes and learner needs in view when evaluating how content, activities, resources, and platform structures should work together.

Bloom's Taxonomy

Considered the relationship between learning activities, cognitive demand, practice, and assessment when supporting course design decisions.

ADDIE

Applied an iterative approach to analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation as platform and curriculum changes moved through development.

Accessibility & Usability

Considered clarity, consistency, navigation, accessibility, and usability as essential components of an effective digital learning environment.

Standardization

Creating consistency that makes learning easier.

One of the most important outcomes of this work was recognizing that consistency is not simply an operational goal—it is part of the learner experience.

Consistent course structures, templates, instructional resources, grading guidance, navigation patterns, and support materials reduce unnecessary cognitive and operational friction.

Standardization also creates a foundation for scalable development. Teams can work more efficiently when expectations, patterns, and quality standards are clear.

Faculty & Team Enablement

Change works better when people are supported through it.

Platform modernization is ultimately a people initiative. Supporting faculty and internal teams was therefore a critical part of the work.

Leadership Takeaway

Technology modernization is ultimately about people.

The strongest technology initiatives connect systems, processes, and people. My role was not simply to understand Moodle—it was to help translate organizational goals into workable processes, consistent experiences, and better learning environments.
