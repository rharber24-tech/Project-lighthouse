Moodle Learning Platform
Modernization

Leading instructional design strategy, project coordination, testing, standardization, and faculty support during a major LMS modernization effort.

Role Instructional Design Leadership
Focus LMS Modernization
Platform Moodle
Strategy Systems · UX · Standardization
The Challenge

Modernizing the platform without losing sight of the learner.

An LMS modernization is more than a technical upgrade. Changes to the platform can affect course structure, navigation, faculty workflows, instructional resources, and ultimately the student experience.

The Moodle 4.x modernization effort required collaboration across Instructional Design, IT, Academics, Content Development, and other organizational stakeholders.

The challenge was to support the transition while maintaining quality, consistency, usability, and instructional integrity across the learning environment.

Strategic Approach

Treating the LMS as a learning ecosystem—not just a platform.

The modernization effort required both technical understanding and instructional design thinking. My approach focused on how changes to the system would affect the people using it.

Coordinate the Work

Worked across teams to organize priorities, identify dependencies, coordinate changes, resolve issues, and keep implementation moving toward shared outcomes.

Test Before Launch

Supported development-environment testing and workflow evaluation to identify potential usability, formatting, and functionality issues before broader implementation.

Standardize the Experience

Applied consistent approaches across courses, templates, instructional resources, and support materials to create a more predictable learning environment.

Protect the Learner Experience

Evaluated platform and curriculum changes through the lens of usability, accessibility, clarity, faculty workflow, and student success.

Instructional Design Strategy

Technology decisions should support learning decisions.

The modernization work provided an opportunity to apply instructional design theory and evidence-informed practices at the system level—not simply inside individual courses.

Backward Design

Kept intended learning outcomes and learner needs in view when evaluating how content, activities, resources, and platform structures should work together.

Bloom's Taxonomy

Considered the relationship between learning activities, cognitive demand, practice, and assessment when supporting course design decisions.

ADDIE

Applied an iterative approach to analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation as platform and curriculum changes moved through development.

Accessibility & Usability

Considered clarity, consistency, navigation, accessibility, and usability as essential components of an effective digital learning environment.

Standardization

Creating consistency that makes learning easier.

One of the most important outcomes of this work was recognizing that consistency is not simply an operational goal—it is part of the learner experience.

Consistent course structures, templates, instructional resources, grading guidance, navigation patterns, and support materials reduce unnecessary cognitive and operational friction.

Standardization also creates a foundation for scalable development. Teams can work more efficiently when expectations, patterns, and quality standards are clear.

Faculty & Team Enablement

Change works better when people are supported through it.

Platform modernization is ultimately a people initiative. Supporting faculty and internal teams was therefore a critical part of the work.

Leadership Takeaway

Technology modernization is ultimately about people.

The most effective technology initiatives connect systems, processes, and people. My role was not simply to understand Moodle—it was to help translate organizational goals into workable processes, consistent experiences, and better learning environments.
